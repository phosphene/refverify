# Arthur C. Parker (1923), Seneca Myths and Folk Tales. Buffalo Historical Society.
# Locus: p. 59, "1. How the World Began"

Beyond the dome we call the sky there is another world. There in the most ancient of times was a fair country where lived the great chief of the up-above-world. In the center of this celestial town grew a celestial tree which blossomed and bore fruit continuously.

The chief's wife had a dream that the tree must be uprooted. The tree was pulled up, leaving a great chasm into the void beneath. The woman peered through the opening and was pushed or fell into the dark gulf below.

Beneath was only a waste of water where lived ducks and water creatures. The birds saw her falling and flew up with joined wings to cushion her descent. The great Snapping Turtle offered his carapace to support her. The creatures dived for earth mud from the deep bottom, and the muskrat brought up a small morsel, which was placed on the turtle's back, where it grew and expanded into Turtle Island.
